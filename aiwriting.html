AI Writing: A Blessing or a Curse?

AI writing tools can enhance productivity and creativity, but uncritical use risks eroding writing skills, authenticity and research integrity.

Suman PathakMechanical & Energy Systems Engineer, Freiburg

Contents

    Contents

      Artificial intelligence writing tools have moved from novelty to routine in a remarkably short span of time. Tools like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini can now draft prose, suggest arguments, summarise documents, and correct grammar — tasks that once required significant human time and skill.

      The question is not whether these tools are useful. They clearly are. The question is: what do we lose when we use them without care?

      What AI Writing Tools Do Well

      AI writing tools excel at reducing friction. For writers of all skill levels, they can:

      • Identify grammatical errors and awkward phrasing
      • Generate topic ideas and structural outlines
      • Suggest improvements in clarity or concision
      • Accelerate routine documentation and correspondence

      In engineering and research contexts specifically, this can be genuinely valuable. Translating technical results into accessible language, or iterating quickly on report structures, are tasks where AI assistance can save hours without compromising intellectual rigour — if used as a tool, not a replacement.

      The Risks: What We Stand to Lose

      The risks are real and worth taking seriously.

      Skill atrophy. Writing is a cognitive exercise. The more we offload it to automated tools, the less we practice the fundamental skill of constructing and communicating an argument. Relying on AI to generate prose, rather than improve it, weakens the very capacity it appears to augment.

      Loss of voice. Good writing has a distinctive voice — a way of framing observations that reflects how a person actually thinks. AI-generated text tends toward the smooth and generic. The more it is used without critical editing, the more writing converges on a homogenised, recognisably artificial style.

      Plagiarism and attribution. When AI generates content based on training data, the provenance of ideas becomes murky. Using AI-produced text without disclosure raises genuine ethical and academic integrity concerns, particularly in research contexts.

      Accuracy and reliability. AI language models can produce confident-sounding errors. In technical fields — energy engineering, policy analysis, scientific writing — an unchecked AI hallucination embedded in a report or paper can be misleading or actively harmful.

      AI in Research: Particular Care Required

      The research sector deserves particular attention. AI writing tools can assist with literature review, synthesis, and drafting. But they can also be misused — to produce low-quality or fraudulent research outputs, obscure limitations, or replace the hard intellectual work of actually understanding a problem.

      The most appropriate use of AI in research is as an editorial assistant, not as a co-author. The thinking, the judgement, the interpretation — these must remain the researcher’s own.

      Using These Tools Responsibly

      The most useful framing is not “AI writing versus human writing” but “AI-assisted writing with maintained critical control.” That means:

      • Using AI to draft, but always editing heavily for accuracy, voice, and clarity
      • Verifying factual claims independently, especially in technical content
      • Being transparent about AI use when disclosure is appropriate
      • Continuing to practise writing without AI assistance regularly

      AI writing tools are likely here to stay. The question is not whether to use them, but how to use them in ways that genuinely augment capability rather than quietly erode it.
