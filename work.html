Energy systems · INATECH, University of Freiburg

District Heating Performance Analysis

As a Student Assistant at INATECH (Department of Sustainable Systems Engineering, University of Freiburg), I monitor and evaluate operational energy consumption and thermodynamic efficiency across district heating systems. The workflow centres on Python-based analytical pipelines: handling large sensor time-series datasets, identifying metering anomalies and modelling temperature regimes.

The core objective is characterising building–network interactions: quantifying distribution heat losses, assessing substation delta-T degradation, and pinpointing underperforming hydraulic segments for network optimisation.

Research · M.Sc. project, University of Freiburg

Heat-Pump Emissions Under Variable Grid CO₂ Intensities: A Regional Study for Germany with PV and CHP

An academic research project completed as part of my M.Sc. programme at the University of Freiburg, alongside my work at INATECH. The study examines how the operational CO₂ emissions of heat pumps change as the carbon intensity of the electricity grid varies, both over time and between regions of Germany.

The analysis includes the effects of photovoltaic (PV) generation and combined heat and power (CHP), which both shape grid carbon intensity at different times and places. It addresses a key question of the energy transition: under what grid conditions do heat pumps actually deliver emission savings over fossil alternatives, and how does that vary by region and season?

Energy system modelling · M.Sc. course project, University of Freiburg

System Requirements for High-Renewable Energy Grids: A Comparative Study

With Mohammad Mahdi Gordali, I built a single-node PyPSA model of the German power system to see what it takes to go from 80% to 100% renewable electricity. It optimises the least-cost mix of solar, wind, batteries, hydrogen and gas over every hour of 2024, under four storage and curtailment scenarios and a CO₂ cap tightened from 10 Mt to net-zero (24 model runs).

Net-zero was reachable in every scenario, but system costs differed 2.4× (€135 vs €318/MWh). Hydrogen as seasonal storage was the biggest lever, and allowing curtailment cut storage needs sharply, showing that Sinn’s (2017) pessimistic storage estimate rests on a no-curtailment assumption.

Simulation · Resilience of Supply Networks, University of Freiburg

Two-Lane Traffic and Resilience: A Nagel–Schreckenberg Cellular Automaton

With Ansh Tak and Mahsa, I extended the Nagel–Schreckenberg traffic model to two lanes with lane changing, then ran parameter sweeps over lane-change probability, the number of cars and the initial lane split. I added a “green-lane priority” rule that turns one lane into a fast lane, and simulated a 10-step lane closure to see how traffic jams form and how the system recovers.

Lane changing and the fast-lane rule raised traffic flow on a lightly used road (up to about +0.13 in the flow measure), but gave no benefit, or a slight loss, once the road was crowded. After the closure, the priority system recovered slightly faster, which we quantified with a time-resolved resilience metric.

Building energy · Case study, Nepal

Earthquake-Resistant Building: Passive & Active Thermal Retrofit Strategies

A case study of thermal retrofitting strategies for earthquake-resistant buildings in Nepal. It analyses passive and active retrofit approaches and their impact on building energy performance and thermal comfort.

Passive strategies assessed include insulation improvements, window upgrades and air-tightening. Active strategies include mechanical heating and cooling systems. The study compares both approaches in the Nepali climate context.
